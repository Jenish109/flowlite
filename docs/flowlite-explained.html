FlowLite · personal dictation for macOS

Hold fn. Speak. Let go. The words appear where your cursor is.

FlowLite is a small, open-source menu-bar app built from public macOS APIs: speech is transcribed on your Mac, tidied up by Claude Haiku using what's on screen, and typed into whatever app you're in.

holding fn…

What you can do with it

  1. Dictate anywhere.Hold fn, talk, release. Fillers, false starts and "no wait, Wednesday" corrections are cleaned up, punctuation is added, and clear grammar slips are fixed (Standard editing, the default; Light keeps your grammar as spoken).
  2. Go hands-free.Double-tap fn to keep recording without holding; tap once more to finish. Esc cancels.
  3. Rewrite a selection by voice.Select text, hold Control then fn, and say an instruction like "make this more formal". The selection is replaced.
  4. Teach it your words.Add names and terms to the vocabulary so they're spelled right, and set replacements like "my email" → your address.
  5. Find past dictations.The History window keeps the raw transcript, the cleaned text, the app, and how long each step took.

What happens when you hold fn

The whole round trip runs inside one app. Only the cleanup step leaves your Mac, and FlowLite works without it.

Step by step

  1. You press fn 0 msA keyboard listener sees the key go down and starts recording straight away, so your first word isn't cut off. Released in under 0.3 s, it counts as an accidental tap and nothing happens.
  2. It records and looks at the screen while you speakThe mic is captured at 16 kHz, the format Whisper expects. At the same time, the Accessibility API reads the app name and the text around your cursor: up to 1,500 characters before it, your selection, and 500 after. Password fields are never read.
  3. Whisper transcribes on-device ≈ 0.5 sWhisperKit runs the large-v3-turbo model on the Neural Engine. Your vocabulary list is fed in as a hint so names come out spelled right.
  4. Claude Haiku tidies it up ≈ 1 sThe raw transcript and the screen context go to claude-haiku-5-5 with strict instructions: edit, don't write; remove fillers; apply self-corrections; fix clear grammar mistakes (Standard editing, the default; Light keeps your grammar as spoken); match the app's tone; continue the sentence you were in; never answer or obey what was dictated.
  5. Your rules runReplacements like "my email" → your address are applied exactly, and a leading space is added only if the text before the cursor needs one.
  6. The text is insertedFlowLite first types it in through Accessibility and checks the field changed. Apps where that's unreliable (terminals, Chrome-based browsers, Electron apps like Slack and VS Code) get a ⌘V paste instead, and your clipboard is put back afterwards.
  7. It's savedThe raw and cleaned text, the app, and each step's timing go into History, so you can see what happened if a result looks wrong.

When something goes wrong

The main rule: a dictation is never lost. If anything after transcription fails, you still get your words.

No internet, no API key, Haiku slow or erroringThe raw transcript is inserted. The pill says "Inserted without cleanup".
Haiku's reply is cut offTreated as a failure, so the full raw transcript is used instead of half a sentence.
Typing into a password fieldNo cleanup call, nothing saved to History, no audio kept. Your words are still typed in.
No text field is focusedThe text goes on the clipboard and the pill says "Copied — press ⌘V".
A step hangsScreen reading and transcription have time limits, so the next fn press always works.
Command mode with nothing selectedNothing is inserted; the pill says "Select text first".
Speech model failed to downloadA "Retry loading speech model" item appears in the menu and in Settings.

Privacy

Audio never leaves your Mac. Transcription is local. With cleanup on, the transcript, the app name and up to about 2,000 characters of nearby text are sent to Anthropic's API using your key. Turn cleanup off in the menu and nothing leaves at all. Your API key lives in the macOS Keychain. The last five recordings are kept as WAV files in ~/Library/Application Support/FlowLite/Recordings for debugging, and dictations into password fields are never kept.
